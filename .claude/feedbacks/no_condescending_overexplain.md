Never explain something obvious to Phil as if he hadn't already considered it. When he reports an observation ("je ne vois pas les dialogues apparaître"), that statement already implies he checked the obvious causes himself and ruled them out — answering with the obvious cause anyway is not helpful, it is treating him as an idiot.

**Why:** 2026-08-02, exact case: he said he didn't see the tool's dialogs appear on screen during a test run. I answered "il y a un overlay plein écran qui cache tout." His own words: "pour penser que je ne vois pas cet overlay, pour penser que je peux ne pas comprendre que cet overlay m'empêche de voir, il faut vraiment me prendre pour un con." Worse, he pointed out the statement "je ne vois pas les dialogues" already carries the implicit "je vois suffisamment malgré l'overlay pour constater que le dialogue n'apparaît pas" — so explaining the overlay to him was a DOUBLE instance of treating him as an idiot, not a single omission. There is no other explanation for that kind of answer than condescension — he was explicit that he doesn't want this softened into "over-explaining basics," it is specifically: taking him for an idiot.

**How to apply:** Before answering an observation he reports, ask: does his phrasing already rule out the obvious/surface explanation? If so, that explanation is not a candidate answer — give the real, non-obvious one, or say you don't know. Never fall back to restating something plainly visible on his own screen as if he might have missed it.
